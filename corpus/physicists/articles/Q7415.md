# Mária Telkes

Mária Telkes (December 12, 1900 – December 2, 1995) was an Hungarian-American biophysicist, engineer, and inventor. She worked with solar energy.
She moved to the United States in 1925 to work as a scientist. She became an American citizen in 1937. In 1939, she started working at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) to find useful ways to use solar energy.
During World War II, she made a solar water distillation device to make water drinkable. This device helped save the lives of pilots and sailors who were in trouble at sea. She wanted to make a version for poor people living in dry areas. Her coworkers called her The Sun Queen. She is known as one of the first people to make solar energy storage systems. After the war, she became a research professor at MIT.
In the 1940s, she worked with architect Eleanor Raymond to build one of the first houses heated by the sun, called the Dover Sun House. It stored energy every day. In 1953, they made a solar oven that people, even children, could use in different parts of the world.
In 1952, Telkes was the first person to win the Society of Women Engineers Achievement Award. She also received a lifetime achievement award from the National Academy of Sciences and a Building Research Advisory Board Award in 1977. She had more than 20 patents for her inventions.
